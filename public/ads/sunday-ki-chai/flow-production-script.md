# BandhanTak — “Sunday Ki Chai”

## Production Format

- Format: Vertical 9:16 story/reel advertisement
- Target duration: 75–80 seconds
- Language: English visual directions; spoken dialogue in Hindi
- Performance: Quiet, realistic and restrained. Let characters pause, breathe and react.
- Branding: BandhanTak appears naturally on the phone once and clearly on the final end card only.

## Master Visual Direction

Use the supplied character-reference image in every scene. Preserve the same faces, ages, hairstyles, skin tones and clothing colors. The father is an Indian man in his late fifties wearing a simple off-white kurta. The daughter is an Indian woman in her late twenties wearing a soft rose floral cotton kurta. The prospective groom is an Indian man in his early thirties wearing a muted olive-green casual kurta-style shirt.

Create a photorealistic, cinematic Indian family drama inside the same tasteful middle-class home. Use natural skin texture, gentle Sunday-morning sunlight, warm cream and wood tones, restrained acting, slow camera movement and shallow depth of field. Keep the emotion sincere rather than melodramatic. Do not use subtitles, generated text, watermarks, wedding glamour, exaggerated crying or fast montage editing. Spoken Hindi must sound conversational and unhurried.

## Scene 1 — The Sunday Ritual (0:00–0:06)

### Visual direction

Begin in near silence inside the kitchen on a peaceful Sunday morning. Show a close shot of chai slowly rising in a steel saucepan. The father pours it carefully into exactly two familiar ceramic cups. Hold for a moment on the steam drifting between the cups. Follow his hands as he carries them toward the dining table. Use two or three long shots rather than quick cuts.

### Audio

Natural room tone, distant birds, the quiet sound of pouring chai and an almost inaudible piano note. No dialogue.

## Scene 2 — The Question (0:06–0:15)

### Visual direction

The daughter sits at the dining table looking at matrimonial profiles on her phone without enthusiasm. The father places one cup beside her and sits opposite her. He glances at the phone, then at her face. Keep the camera still in a warm two-shot. After speaking, he waits instead of immediately reacting.

### Spoken dialogue

**पापा:** “कल वाला लड़का अच्छा था… फिर मना क्यों कर दिया?”

**बेटी:** “लड़के में कोई कमी नहीं थी, पापा।”

**पापा:** “फिर?”

## Scene 3 — What She Is Afraid Of (0:15–0:27)

### Visual direction

The daughter locks her phone and looks at the two cups. Her thumb gently traces the rim of her cup. Leave a full silent beat before she speaks. During the second line, move very slowly toward her face. She does not cry; her eyes only become slightly heavy. The father’s reassuring smile fades into quiet understanding. End with the daughter placing her hand over his hand on the table.

### Spoken dialogue

**बेटी:** “मम्मी के जाने के बाद… हर रविवार की चाय हमने साथ पी है।”

Pause for two seconds.

**बेटी:** “शादी से डर नहीं लगता… बस ये सोचकर डर लगता है कि आपकी रविवार वाली चाय अकेली हो जाएगी।”

Pause. The father forces a small reassuring smile.

**पापा:** “पगली… मैं संभाल लूँगा।”

## Scene 4 — A Father’s Quiet Search (0:27–0:33)

### Visual direction

It is late evening in the same house. The father collects the two empty cups from the table. He washes one, then pauses with the other in his hand. Later, he sits alone under a warm lamp and opens BandhanTak on his phone. Show only a brief, believable glimpse of the service; do not turn the shot into a product demonstration.

### Audio

Soft room ambience and a restrained piano motif. No dialogue.

## Scene 5 — The Video That Feels Different (0:33–0:44)

### Visual direction

On the phone is a simple self-recorded video from the prospective groom. It is not polished or glamorous. He is in his family kitchen preparing chai while his parents move naturally in the background. He looks into the phone camera with a calm, honest expression. Let him speak at a relaxed pace with a small pause in the middle.

### Spoken dialogue inside the video profile

**लड़का:** “मैं शादी में सिर्फ एक जीवनसाथी नहीं ढूँढ़ रहा।”

Short pause.

**लड़का:** “मैं चाहता हूँ कि उसके माता-पिता भी… मेरे अपने रहें।”

His mother calls gently from outside the frame.

**लड़के की माँ:** “चाय ठंडी हो रही है।”

The boy smiles naturally and looks away from the camera.

**लड़का:** “आ रहा हूँ, माँ।”

## Scene 6 — Look at the Person (0:44–0:51)

### Visual direction

The father replays the last moment and smiles. He walks to the daughter’s doorway without excitement or pressure and silently offers her the phone. She looks at him before accepting it. As the video plays, stay on her face rather than showing the phone again. A very small, relieved smile appears.

### Spoken dialogue

**पापा:** “इस बार बायोडाटा नहीं… इसकी सोच देख।”

## Scene 7 — The First Cup Together (0:51–0:58)

### Visual direction

Some time later, the prospective groom visits their home for the first time. Avoid a formal matrimonial-meeting setup. The three sit naturally at the same dining table. The father pours chai. The young man takes one sip, appreciates it honestly, and the tension breaks into a gentle laugh. This small moment shows that the relationship developed gradually rather than instantly.

### Spoken dialogue

**लड़का:** “आपकी रविवार वाली चाय के बारे में बहुत सुना है।”

**पापा:** “पहले पी लो… फिर तारीफ़ करना।”

## Scene 8 — The First Sunday After Marriage (0:58–1:05)

### Visual direction

Return to the opening composition several months later. A subtle wedding photograph may sit softly out of focus in the room. The father opens the cupboard and automatically reaches for two cups. He stops. After a long beat, he returns one cup to the shelf and carries a single cup to the silent dining table. Match the framing from Scene 1 so the absence is immediately felt.

### Audio

No music for the first few seconds. Let the quiet room be heard. Then the doorbell rings.

## Scene 9 — Two Cups Become Three (1:05–1:14)

### Visual direction

The father opens the door. The daughter and her husband stand outside in simple everyday clothes, carrying a small breakfast packet and three ceramic cups. Do not use slow-motion or dramatic tears. The husband lifts the cups slightly with a warm smile. The father’s eyes become moist, but he answers with a smile. The daughter quietly takes his arm as they enter.

### Spoken dialogue

**लड़का:** “पापा… रविवार की चाय हमारे बिना?”

The daughter shows him the three cups.

**बेटी:** “अब दो नहीं… तीन कप निकला करेंगे।”

## Scene 10 — Final Emotional Payoff and Brand (1:14–1:19)

### Visual direction

Place exactly three cups together on the wooden table in sharp focus. In the softly blurred background, the father, daughter and husband share a natural laugh. Hold the image long enough for the audience to feel the moment. Fade gently into the supplied end-card background. Overlay the original BandhanTak logo without altering it.

### End-card copy

**BandhanTak**

**रिश्ता सिर्फ दो लोगों का नहीं होता।**

### Voice-over

“बायोडाटा से आगे बढ़िए। इंसान को उसकी वीडियो से समझिए—बंधनतक पर।”

## Editing and Music Notes

- Do not cut immediately after a dialogue. Preserve one to two seconds for reactions.
- Use slow push-ins, locked two-shots and gentle match cuts between the cups.
- Keep the two-cup motif visible in the beginning and the one-cup moment before the final reveal.
- Let the music disappear when the father sits with one cup. Bring back warm strings only when the door opens.
- Keep the logo end card visible for at least four seconds.
- Add the real logo and final typography during editing. Do not ask the video model to generate the logo or Hindi text.
